## Why Khoim
Most places in Goa have an official spelling and a Konkani name, and the two often don't match. Canacona is काणकोण, written Kannkonn in Romi. Many official spellings come from Portuguese-era records and are what you see on boards, maps and bills.

If you grew up here you know both. A Goan kid growing up in Pune or Toronto often doesn't, and neither does someone who moved here last year.

Khoim puts both on one map. It's non-profit and the data is open. Later we want people from each taluka recording the names, because the same village can sound different depending on who's saying it.

## Where the names come from
District, taluka and village names and boundaries come from the Local Government Directory (LGD) of the Government of India, spelt exactly as the government spells them.

The Konkani names for districts and talukas come from the district websites of Goa and published Konkani sources. For Bardez, Tiswadi, Salcete, Quepem and South Goa those sources differ, and each of those cards says so. Dharbandora shows only its official name until the Directorate of Official Language confirms the Konkani.

The pronunciation guides are our own rough respellings. No Konkani speaker has checked them yet.

Village names in Konkani aren't up yet. Each will go up once someone from that area has reviewed it.

## A note on scripts
Konkani is written in Devanagari, the official script under the Goa, Daman and Diu Official Language Act, 1987, and in Romi, the Roman script used in church, in tiatr and in many homes. Marathi can also be used for official work in Goa. Khoim shows Devanagari and Romi side by side and doesn't pick between them.

## Collaborator note (from Shashank, about 300 words)

**Khoim (खंय): a public map of Goa's place names**

Khoim is a non-profit, mobile-first map of Goa. It sets each place's official spelling next to its Konkani name in Devanagari and Romi, with a plain guide to saying it. My team at Pangolin Marketing is building it.

Most official spellings come from Portuguese-era records, so someone new to Goa can live in Canacona for a year and never learn it is काणकोण. Many Goans raised outside Goa are in the same place. There is no single public page where the official name, the Konkani name and the way people say it sit together.

**What exists.** A draft map of Goa, its three districts including Kushavati, all 12 talukas and 429 villages from the Local Government Directory. Seven taluka names are well sourced. Four have sources that disagree. Dharbandora is waiting on the Directorate of Official Language. The pronunciation guides are my own rough respellings and nobody has checked them yet. Villages show only their official names.

**Where I need help.** Konkani speakers from each taluka to review village names. Later, recordings of people saying their own place names, with more than one voice per place, because the same name can sound different in different talukas and families.

**What contributors get.** Your name on every entry you review, record or supply. You can correct or withdraw your contribution at any time. The data stays open for anyone to reuse with credit.

**How decisions are made.** Government records first. Nothing is marked reviewed until a Konkani speaker has reviewed it. Khoim records names and does not pick between scripts. I am forming a small advisory group from different communities and the diaspora to settle disputed entries. My team is building the site, but the names are for Konkani reviewers to settle.

Could you look at the draft and tell me what's wrong with it? Even a quick look would help.

Shashank
Co-founder, Pangolin Marketing
